## Stage 1 — throughput, and where 1 MP/s actually falls

  quantizer, same box; both control bands are ≤ 5 %, so this is not close to noise).
* **SVT-AV1 presets 9-13 are aliases in all-intra.** The library prints

  all-intra preset is M9, for both the C encoder and the Rust port.
* `zenrav1e` speed 6 lands at **0.98 MP/s** in the 3-rep screen and **0.96 MP/s** in the 7-rep
  ladder — it misses the bar by 2-4 %, outside the 0.35 % control band for that encoder but
  close enough that a different image or size would flip it. Stated, not rounded away.

### 1b. The size axis — and why a bare "MP/s" is not a number

Four sizes per encoder per preset (64², 256², 1024², 2048² photo; 64², 256², 1024², 1856²
screen), each a Lanczos3 downscale of one source, so pixel count is the axis that moves.
Full per-cell medians in `xbench_stage1_2026-08-01.samples.tsv`; the fit and its residuals in
`xbench_stage1_fit_2026-08-01.tsv`.

At the qualifying modes, on photographic content:

| encoder | mode | 64² | 256² | 1024² | 2048² | alpha (ms) | beta (ms/MP) | max fit residual | log-log k |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| svt-c | p1 | 0.343 | 0.799 | 1.553 | 1.995 | 65.54 | 491.2 | **466 %** | 0.758 |
| svt-rust | p4 | 0.503 | 0.880 | 1.308 | 1.459 | 37.10 | 679.8 | **390 %** | 0.859 |
| zenav1-aom | cpu6 | 0.348 | 0.825 | 2.087 | 2.787 | 61.80 | 348.5 | **437 %** | 0.715 |
| **libaom-c** | **cpu1** | **0.328** | **0.890** | **1.753** | **2.347** | **65.03** | **416.2** | **434 %** | **0.728** |
| zenrav1e | s7 | 0.763 | 1.146 | 1.793 | 2.218 | 43.91 | 444.8 | **752 %** | 0.846 |

(MP/s per size; alpha/beta from least squares on `total = alpha + beta * pixels`.)

**The requested alpha/beta fit is reported, and it is a bad model — that is the finding.**
The max residual column is the whole story: a straight line through the ladder over-predicts the
64² cell by 4-8x. Encode time is **sub-linear in pixel count** — the log-log exponent
`k` (time ~ pixels^k) is 0.70-0.97 everywhere, never 1.0. Per-pixel cost *falls* as the frame
grows, so:

* A single "MP/s" figure is meaningless without the size it was taken at. At the qualifying
  modes the same encoder ranges over **4-6x in MP/s** across the ladder (`zenav1-aom` cpu-used 6:
  0.348 MP/s at 64², 2.787 MP/s at 2048² — a factor of 8).
* The fitted `alpha` should NOT be read as "fixed per-call cost". It is what a straight line
  needs in order to pass near the big points, and it exceeds the entire measured 64² encode time
  in most rows. The honest statement of fixed overhead is the measured tiny cell itself:
  e.g. `svt-c` preset 13 encodes 64x64 in **0.182 ms** and 2048x2048 in **17.36 ms** — 1024x the
  pixels for only 95x the time, so the fast end is sub-linear too (k = 0.705).
* **1 MP/s is therefore a size-conditional bar.** `zenav1-aom` cpu-used 5 is 0.92 MP/s at 1 MP
  but **1.18 MP/s at 4 MP** — it fails the bar at 1 MP and passes it at 4 MP. Likewise
  `zenrav1e` speed 6: 0.96 at 1 MP, 1.15 at 4 MP. And `libaom-c` cpu-used 0: 0.70 MP/s at 1 MP,
  **0.85 MP/s at 4 MP** — still short, but the gap halves. The frontier table above is pinned
  to the 1 MP cell and says so.

**Is the sub-linearity an artifact of building the ladder by downscaling?** Partly — checked,
not assumed. Downscaling smooths detail, and the small cells are downscaled harder, which would
make them look *cheaper* per pixel, i.e. it works against the observed trend. A native-crop
control (same source, centre crop at native resolution, no resampling, `svt-c` preset 1,
3 timed encodes each):

| size | Lanczos3 downscale | native centre crop | native per-pixel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 256² | 84.26 ms | 123.20 ms | 1.880 µs/px |
| 1024² | 697.78 ms | 1155.91 ms | 1.102 µs/px |

Native content is 1.5-1.7x more expensive than the downscale at the same size — so the ladder
*understates* absolute cost at the large end — and the sub-linearity survives on native content
too (1.880 -> 1.102 µs/px from 256² to 1024², a 1.7x fall). The trend is real; the downscale
ladder is a lower bound on how strong it is.

### 1c. Content changes throughput more than one preset step does

Same encoders, same sizes, screen content (`gb82-sc/imac_dark`, centre-square crops) instead of
photographs, at 1 MP:

| encoder | mode | photo MP/s | screen MP/s | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| svt-c | p1 | 1.553 | 0.278 | 5.6x |
| svt-rust | p4 | 1.308 | 0.520 | 2.5x |
| zenav1-aom | cpu6 | 2.087 | 0.403 | 5.2x |
| **libaom-c** | **cpu1** | **1.753** | **0.038** | **45.6x** |
| zenrav1e | s7 | 1.793 | 0.774 | 2.3x |

**Not one of the five qualifying modes clears 1 MP/s on screen content.** For `svt-c` the photo
frontier (preset 1) runs at 0.28 MP/s on a screenshot — the screen-content frontier is
**preset 3** (1.19 MP/s; preset 2 is 0.55), two presets faster. Any ">= 1 MP/s" claim that
does not name its content class is unfalsifiable.

`libaom-c` is the extreme case and deserves its own paragraph, because 45.6x is not a content
factor of the kind the other four show. Its screen frontier is **cpu-used 5**, at
1.0000 MP/s — *four* modes faster than its photo frontier, where `svt-c`'s moves by two (the
other three encoders' screen frontiers were not screened)
(`xbench_stage1_libaomc_screen_2026-08-01.tsv`, same driver, median of 3):

| cpu-used | 9 | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MP/s, 1 MP screen | 81.84 | 14.20 | 6.96 | 3.69 | **1.000** | 0.688 |
| coded bytes | 70 599 | 71 638 | 63 181 | 72 989 | 61 804 | 61 035 |

and at cpu-used 1 the same cell takes **27.31 s** (0.0384 MP/s) for **39 406 bytes** — 36 %
fewer bits than cpu-used 5 buys, at 26x the time. libaom's allintra defaults leave palette and
IntraBC ON and let its own screen-content detector arm them; that search is what both the bytes
and the seconds are.

**An anomaly, measured and not diagnosed:** the 1 MP screen cell at cpu-used 1 (27.31 s) costs
**4.4x the wall time of the 3.3x-LARGER 1856² screen cell** (6.18 s) at the same 0.30 bpp —
26 µs/px vs 1.8 µs/px. That is not sub-linearity, it is a different search configuration on the
two frames, and it is why the libaom-c screen row of the alpha/beta fit comes out at a 65 865 %
residual and `k = 0.53`: the model is being fitted to a non-monotone series. **Do not read a
libaom-c screen MP/s off a fit** — the two measured cells are in
`xbench_stage1_libaomc_2026-08-01.raw.tsv` and disagree about what "per pixel" means. What
causes the two frames to be configured differently was not established here.

## Stage 2 — compression efficiency at the qualifying modes

Each encoder at its **slowest mode that clears 1 MP/s** (the RD-best point of the qualifying
set): `svt-c` p1, `svt-rust` p4, `zenav1-aom` cpu-used 6, `libaom-c` cpu-used 1,
`zenrav1e` speed 7. 13 images across
three classes (8 CID22 512², 2 CLIC 1024² centre-square, 3 gb82-sc screenshots centre-cropped at
native resolution — no Kodak, no gradients), 14 quantizer points spanning each encoder's **full**
native range, so the aggressive end is sampled at least as densely as the high-quality end
(measured SSIMULACRA2 coverage: −64 … +92). 728 cells, **0 encode failures, 0 decode failures**.
BD-rate is PCHIP over log10(bpp) vs the metric on the overlapping quality interval.
Raw: `xbench_stage2_2026-08-01.tsv`; full tables: `xbench_stage2_bdrate_2026-08-01.txt`.

Butteraugli agrees with SSIMULACRA2 on the ordering everywhere except `zenrav1e`, which is
noticeably worse under butteraugli than under SSIMULACRA2 on photographs (+20.95 % vs +13.15 %
median) — i.e. `zenrav1e` at speed 7 buys some of its SSIMULACRA2 position with distortion
butteraugli penalises. No veto fires on the others.

**`svt-c` preset 1 wins every one of the 13 images against the original four.** That is a
direct consequence of Stage 1: being ~5x faster than the Rust port at matched preset, the C
encoder can afford preset 1 inside the same 1 MP/s budget while the port can only afford
preset 4.

**Adding libaom C changes the winner.** At its own qualifying mode `libaom-c` is +3.85 %
overall — half of `svt-c@p4`'s deficit and the only arm that **beats** `svt-c` p1 on screen
content (−15.32 % SSIMULACRA2, −15.80 % butteraugli-3n) and on butteraugli max-norm overall
(−2.34 %). On photographs it still trails `svt-c` p1 by +4.18 %. Same reason as before: both C
encoders get almost their whole ladder inside the budget, so this is close to a
slowest-vs-slowest comparison.

### The control that matters: is the Rust SVT port's deficit *coding* or *throughput*?

`svt-c @p4` is the **same C encoder run at the Rust port's qualifying preset** — an extra arm
run purely as this control. Its BD-rate is **+8.25 %**; `svt-rust` at that same preset is
**+8.41 %**. The two are 0.16 pp apart, and per-image they track to within 1.2 pp on 12 of 13
images.

> The Rust SVT port's entire 8.4 % compression deficit at the 1 MP/s budget is a **throughput**
> deficit, not a coding deficit. At preset 4 the port's output is **byte-identical** to the C
> encoder's (whole-stream sha256 on the calibration cell —
> `xbench_svt_byteidentity_2026-08-01.tsv`; also identical at presets 7, 8, 9, 13, differing at
> 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, which is where the 62-commit version skew and the port's documented pinned
> near-ties live). It loses only because it is 4-8x slower per preset and therefore has to run
> three presets faster to fit the budget.

### The same control for the aom port: `libaom-c @cpu6`

*(Added 2026-08-01. This is the arm the first version of this document said was missing.)*

`libaom-c @cpu6` is the **same C encoder at the port's qualifying cpu-used**, driven through a
line-for-line transcription of the very config call the port already uses for its
sequence-header bootstrap. So the two differ in nothing but which encoder produced the frame.

> **On photographic content the port has NO coding deficit against libaom.** Nine of the ten
> photo/photo-hr images are bit-exact reproductions of what the C encoder emits, and the tenth
> (`cid22_6292444`) costs **0.37 pp**. The port's entire photographic gap to `svt-c` is a
> **throughput** gap, exactly as the Rust SVT port's was — and the size of it is legible
> directly: libaom C at its own qualifying mode is +4.18 % on photos, at the port's cpu-used 6
> it is +15.82 %, so **being unable to afford cpu-used 1 costs +11.64 pp**. That is the whole
> photographic story.

`cid22_6292444` is the one real coding divergence on photographs, and it is small and
consistent: at every q from 10 to 58 the port emits **0.25-2.52 % fewer bytes** at a slightly
lower SSIMULACRA2 (−0.00 to −2.46), which nets out to 0.37 pp worse BD-rate. It is not a
tie-break or a rounding artifact — it reproduces at all 13 non-degenerate quantizer points —
and it is not yet localized. (Cells: `xbench_stage2_libaomc_cpu6_2026-08-01.tsv` and
`xbench_stage2_aomport_cpu6_2026-08-01.tsv`.)

**On screen content the port DOES have a coding deficit, and it is enormous:** +131.77 % vs the
C encoder's +18.34 % at the same cpu-used 6 — a **113 pp** gap that has nothing to do with
speed. Concretely, at matched preset and near-equal quality:

| image | q | zenav1-aom@now bytes | libaom-c bytes | delta | ss2 (port / C) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sc_terminal | 10 | 66 262 | 23 673 | **+179.9 %** | 91.84 / 91.89 |
| sc_terminal | 30 | 44 666 | 16 631 | **+168.6 %** | 83.90 / 84.43 |
| sc_imac_dark | 10 | 113 833 | 44 900 | **+153.5 %** | 90.49 / 90.54 |
| sc_imac_dark | 30 | 77 168 | 30 719 | **+151.2 %** | 79.13 / 80.89 |
| sc_codec_wiki | 10 | 13 596 | 10 083 | +34.8 % | 92.22 / 92.28 |

The cause is named in the next section: libaom's ALLINTRA defaults leave palette and IntraBC ON
and let libaom's own detector arm them; the port's `ToggleKnobs::default()` — the path the
harness measures — has both OFF. This is a **search-capability** difference between two
configurations of the same algorithm, not two encoders disagreeing about how to value a block.

*Caveat on which port build.* The originally published `zenav1-aom` row was measured at
`ea3bed3`; the byte-identity run necessarily uses the port as it is today (`7b560c6`), and
those are not the same encoder — re-running the same 182 cells, **101 of 182 byte counts
differ** (by 0.1-1.4 %; commits `99e3d96` KB-24/25, `a264c50` KB-26, `be2108f` KB-29). Rather
than restate a published row from a different build, `zenav1-aom@now` is added alongside it. The
two land in the same place (+20.79 % vs +20.72 % overall, +15.89 % vs +15.82 % photo), so the
decomposition does not depend on which is used; every claim above that invokes byte identity
quotes the re-measured one.

### The screen-content result is a TOOLS-AVAILABILITY result, not an RD result

The 2-3x screen-content gap for `zenav1-aom` and `zenrav1e` is dominated by which
screen-content tools are ON in each encoder's default still configuration at these modes:

* **SVT-AV1** (both C and the Rust port) runs its screen-content detection and palette path at
  presets ≤ 7 — the library prints `Screen-content detection and tools are disabled for all-intra
  coding at M8 and above` only above that, so at p1/p4 the tools are live.
* **`libaom-c`**: its ALLINTRA config defaults leave **palette and IntraBC ON**, armed per frame
  by libaom's own screen-content detector. The driver sets no coding-tool control at all, so
  this is genuinely the default path — and it is why `libaom-c` is the only arm here that beats
  `svt-c` p1 on screenshots.
* **`zenav1-aom`**: the harness drives the port through `port_encode` = `ToggleKnobs::default()`,
  in which the port's **palette and IntraBC RD searches are OFF**. That is the port's default
  path, so it is what the headline number measures — but it is a search-capability gap, not the
  port valuing blocks badly.
* **`zenrav1e`**: `PaletteMode` defaults to `Off` in this fork, and IntraBC is behind a
  default-off `SpeedSettings.prediction.intrabc`.

## Stage 3 — quality-target accuracy

**None of the four encoders has a native "encode to SSIMULACRA2 = T" mode**, so all four are
driven by the **same external search**, implemented once in `scripts/xbench.py::target_search`
and shaped like a real target-quality implementation (cf. zenavif's `encode_rgb8_with_target`):
seed from a fixed anchor line in *normalized* quantizer space, one offset probe for a secant
slope, then bracketed secant, stopping on |achieved − target| <= 1.0, on the bracket collapsing
to one quantizer step, or at 8 encodes. Identical for every encoder — so any difference below is
a property of that encoder's own quality-vs-quantizer curve, not of a different search.

13 images x 5 targets (SSIMULACRA2 50/60/70/80/90) x 5 encoders = 325 searches, at the same
qualifying modes as Stage 2. Raw: `xbench_stage3_2026-08-01.tsv` and
`xbench_stage3_libaomc_2026-08-01.tsv`.

| encoder | n | median \|err\| | p90 \|err\| | max \|err\| | median encodes | mean encodes | converged | misses |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| svt-c p1 | 65 | 0.514 | 1.039 | 1.707 | 3 | 3.25 | 89.2 % | 7 |
| svt-rust p4 | 65 | 0.409 | 1.046 | 1.935 | 3 | 3.23 | 89.2 % | 7 |
| **libaom-c cpu1** | 65 | 0.478 | 1.166 | **3.819** | 3 | 3.35 | **83.1 %** | **11** |
| zenav1-aom cpu6 | 65 | 0.369 | 0.855 | 1.120 | 3 | 3.06 | 96.9 % | 2 |
| **zenrav1e s7** | 65 | **0.353** | **0.731** | **0.914** | 3 | 3.28 | **100 %** | **0** |

All five land a ±1 SSIMULACRA2 target in a **median of 3 encodes**, and no search ever failed
because a target was out of reach — **zero** `above_max_quality` / `below_min_quality` /
`encode_error` outcomes across all 325 searches. Every one of the 27 misses is
`band_not_reached`.

**`libaom-c` is the hardest of the five to target, and for a second reason.** Its quantizer
granularity is unremarkable (1.911 SSIMULACRA2 per step, against `svt-c`'s 1.915 — same scale,
same story), yet it converges on only 83.1 % and its worst case is 3.8 SSIMULACRA2 off after
spending the full 8-encode budget. Seven of its 11 misses are on screen content, so the
suspect cell is `sc_codec_wiki`, target 70. Probing five consecutive quantizers there
(`xbench_stage3_libaomc_qprobe_2026-08-01.tsv`):

| cq | 47 | 48 | 49 | 50 | 51 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SSIMULACRA2 | 76.253 | 74.555 | 73.819 | **70.610** | **74.445** |
| bpp | 0.0277 | 0.0276 | 0.0267 | 0.0268 | 0.0250 |

**The curve is not monotone.** Raising the quantizer from 50 to 51 *raises* quality by 3.8
points while lowering the rate. No bracketed search can converge across that — the bracket it
maintains is not a bracket. `sc_imac_dark` shows the same sign flip, smaller (58.700 at q50,
58.735 at q51). This is a real property of libaom's cpu-used 1 screen-content path (the deep
palette/IntraBC search re-decides block-level tooling between adjacent quantizers), not a
search-implementation artifact, and it is a different failure mode from the granularity one
below: granularity means the band is *narrower than a step*, non-monotonicity means the band
can be **stepped over in both directions**.

### Why the misses happen, and why `zenrav1e` has none: quantizer granularity

Measured from the Stage-2 grid — mean |ΔSSIMULACRA2| per **one** quantizer step, in the
SSIMULACRA2 40-75 band where every miss occurred:

| encoder | quantizer scale | SSIMULACRA2 per step |
|---|---|---|
| svt-c | 0..63 | **1.915** |
| svt-rust | 0..63 | **1.929** |
| **libaom-c** | 0..63 | **1.911** |
| zenav1-aom | 0..63 | **1.861** |
| zenrav1e | 0..255 | **0.503** |

On a 0..63 scale one quantizer step is worth ~1.9 SSIMULACRA2 in that band — **wider than the
±1.0 target band**. So for those encoders a ±1 target is sometimes *structurally* unreachable:
the search brackets it to adjacent integers and neither lands inside. `zenrav1e`'s 0..255 scale
moves 0.50 per step, four times finer, and it converges on 65/65. That is the whole difference —
not search quality, not curve smoothness. (Per class the step is 1.82-1.87 on photographs,
1.52-1.78 on photo-hr and **2.23-2.29 on screenshots** for every 0..63 encoder, which is why
the screen class dominates the miss list.)

The pattern matches: 26 of the 27 misses are at target 50, 60 or 70 (the steep part of the
curve); at target 90 all five encoders converge **65/65**, and the only miss above 70 anywhere
is `libaom-c`'s non-monotone `sc_codec_wiki` cell.

| target | svt-c | svt-rust | libaom-c | zenav1-aom | zenrav1e |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 10/13 | 8/13 | 7/13 | 11/13 | 13/13 |
| 60 | 11/13 | 11/13 | 11/13 | 13/13 | 13/13 |
| 70 | 11/13 | 13/13 | 11/13 | 13/13 | 13/13 |
| 80 | 13/13 | 13/13 | 12/13 | 13/13 | 13/13 |
| 90 | 13/13 | 13/13 | 13/13 | 13/13 | 13/13 |

Practical reading: if you need tight quality targeting at aggressive web quality (SSIMULACRA2
50-70) — where the bytes actually matter — the SVT encoders and the aom port need either a
finer quantizer control than their CLI-level 0..63 scale, or a band wider than ±1. `zenrav1e`
gets it for free from its scale.

### Cost of targeting

Median 3 encodes everywhere, mean 3.06-3.35, p90 4. At the qualifying modes that is roughly
3x the single-encode time in Stage 1 — e.g. `svt-c` p1 on a 1 MP photo: ~0.68 s per encode,
so ~2 s to hit a target. `zenrav1e` needs marginally more encodes on screen content (mean 3.9)
than the others (3.1-3.3); `libaom-c` needs the most (mean 4.40 on screen, against 2.90-3.08 on
photographs) — the non-monotone cells above are where the extra encodes go.

## What surprised me

1. **`svt-c` gets to use preset 1 inside a 1 MP/s budget** — and so does `libaom-c` (cpu-used 1;
   only cpu-used 0 is excluded). I expected the throughput bar to push every encoder into its
   fast tier. It pushes `zenav1-aom` to cpu-used 6 and `zenrav1e` to speed 7, but **both C
   encoders keep their slowest-but-one mode**. That, not any RD cleverness, is most of why they
   win Stage 2.
2. **The Rust SVT port's compression deficit is 100 % a speed deficit.** `svt-c@p4` +8.25 % vs
   `svt-rust@p4` +8.41 % — 0.16 pp apart. I expected some coding difference to show up in the
   BD-rate; there is none to find. Every point of its +8.4 % is bought back by making the port
   faster, not by changing a single RD decision.
3. **The aom port's is too — on photographs — and the demonstration is stronger than a
   near-tie.** *(Added 2026-08-01.)* `zenav1-aom` and `libaom-c` at matched cpu-used 6 are
   **byte-identical on 9 of the 10 photo/photo-hr images at all 14 quantizer points**, so their
   BD-rates are not merely close, they are the same number. The port's photographic deficit is
   entirely that it runs at 2.13 MP/s where libaom runs at 22.93 and therefore cannot buy
   cpu-used 1. What I did *not* expect: **on screen content the same matched-preset comparison
   shows a genuine 113 pp coding gap** (+18.34 % vs +131.77 %) — so the port's deficit is
   throughput on photographs and capability on screenshots, and the single "+20.79 %" headline
   was averaging two unrelated causes.
4. **`total = alpha + beta*pixels` is simply the wrong model** for still-picture encode time —
   by 4-8x at 64x64, for every encoder and every preset. Encode time goes as `pixels^0.70..0.97`.
   I went in expecting to report a clean intercept and slope; the honest answer is that the
   intercept is a fitting artifact and MP/s has to be quoted with its frame size.
5. **Content moves throughput more than two preset steps do.** Every qualifying mode fails the
   1 MP/s bar on screen content, by 2.3-5.6x — and `libaom-c` by **45.6x**, which is not a
   content factor at all but a whole second encoder configuration switching itself on. A
   throughput SLA stated without a content class is not checkable.
6. **A ±1 SSIMULACRA2 target is below the quantizer resolution** of four of the five encoders
   in the 40-75 band (1.9 SSIMULACRA2 per step on a 0..63 scale). `zenrav1e`'s only structural
   advantage in this whole study is that its scale is 4x finer. *(Added 2026-08-01: `libaom-c`
   shows that granularity is not the only obstacle — on screen content at cpu-used 1 its
   quality-vs-quantizer curve is **non-monotone**, and a bracketed search cannot converge on a
   curve that goes the wrong way.)*
7. **Arming the aom port's palette search made files bigger, and its IntraBC search produced
   streams both reference decoders reject.** I expected the screen-content gap to be a
   switched-off-tool story; it is a not-yet-working-tool story. *(Updated 2026-08-01: the
   IntraBC half was a real conformance bug — KB-29, six roots, now fixed and re-measured
   conformant at −49 % bytes. The palette half stands: it still grows the file. What now
   blocks IntraBC is its ~43x encode time, not its output.)*
8. **And that ~43x turned out to be a property of the port, not of the tool.** *(Added
   2026-08-01.)* libaom C, at the *same* cpu-used 6 on the *same* screenshot, runs its palette
   and IntraBC searches — armed by its own detector, no flags — and lands a **smaller and
   better** file in **126.9 ms** where the port's armed path takes **70.8 s**. I had written
   the screen-content gap up as "the tools are outside the throughput budget". They are not.
   They are outside *this port's* throughput budget.

## What is NOT measured here

Stated plainly so nothing above is over-read:

* **Multi-threaded throughput.** Everything is single-threaded. SVT-AV1 and `zenrav1e` both
  scale across cores; `zenav1-aom` has no threading. A multi-core comparison would look
  completely different and is not in this data.
* **10-bit, 4:4:4, 4:2:2, monochrome, non-64-aligned frames, animation.** 8-bit 4:2:0,
  64-aligned squares only. (`svt-rust` additionally requires preset >= 6 for non-64-aligned
  dimensions, which this grid never exercises.)
* ~~**`zenav1-aom` vs libaom C.**~~ **Measured 2026-08-01** — see "The same control for the aom
  port: `libaom-c @cpu6`" above. The decomposition now exists: throughput on photographs
  (byte-identical at matched preset), capability on screen content (113 pp at matched preset).
* **libaom-c Stage 3 at cpu-used 6.** Stage 3 was run for `libaom-c` at its qualifying mode
  (cpu-used 1) only. A matched-preset Stage-3 arm was not run.
* **The cpu-used 0 screen size-ladder for `libaom-c`.** Phase A (which decides the frontier)
  covers cpu-used 0 at 1 MP; the bracket ladder was stopped before its two screen cells, which
  were projecting ~35 minutes for rows no table uses.
* **What makes libaom configure the 1 MP and 1856² screen frames so differently** (27.31 s vs
  6.18 s at the same bpp, §1c). Measured and reproducible; not diagnosed.
* **A stock (non-oracle) libaom build.** The libaom under test is the repo's pinned oracle:
  Release, `CONFIG_MULTITHREAD=0`, and `-ffp-contract=off` on every TU. The last of those is a
  deviation from a stock aarch64 build, which would fuse `a*b+c`; its cost in wall time was not
  measured, so treat libaom-c's throughput as a lower bound by at most a few per cent. NEON,
  dotprod, i8mm and SVE are all enabled and runtime-dispatched (`aom_config.h`, quoted in the
  arm's .meta).
* **`aomenc`, the command-line tool.** The driver links libaom directly, so nothing here is a
  statement about `aomenc`'s CLI or its defaults.
* **Any non-default tuning.** Every encoder runs its own default still configuration at the
  chosen preset. No `--tune`, no quantizer matrices, no variance boost, no screen-content
  forcing (except the isolated aom A/B, which is reported separately and excluded from the
  headline).
* **Decode speed.** Only encode is timed.
* **Larger than 4.19 MP, and smaller than 64x64.**
* **`svt-c` version skew.** The C tree is 62 upstream commits past the v4.2.0 that `svt-rust`
  ports. Measured per preset on the calibration cell, they are byte-identical at presets 4, 7,
  8, 9 and 13 and differ at 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6
  (`xbench_svt_byteidentity_2026-08-01.tsv`). Preset 4 — the port's qualifying mode and the
  control arm's mode — is identical, so the skew did not move the reported comparison; but it
  is real, and `svt-c`'s own qualifying mode (preset 1) is one of the diverging ones.
* **Per-image variance beyond n=13.** The BD-rate medians come from 13 images (8 photo, 2
  photo-hr, 3 screen). Per-image spread is wide (e.g. `zenav1-aom` ranges +9.16 % to +30.86 %
  on photos); the per-image table is in `xbench_stage2_bdrate_2026-08-01.txt`.
* **Stage-2 `enc_ms`.** Informational only — two encode arms ran concurrently on the box during
  part of Stage 2/3, so those timings are not clean. Every timing number quoted in this document
  comes from Stage 1 or the control runs, which ran alone.
