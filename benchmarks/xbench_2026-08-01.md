# Four AV1 still-picture encoders at >= 1 MP/s — measured

**2026-08-01.** Provenance, commits, colour handling, timed regions and exact commands:
[`xbench_2026-08-01.meta`](xbench_2026-08-01.meta). Harness:
[`benchmarks/xbench`](xbench) + [`scripts/xbench.py`](../scripts/xbench.py).

| label | what it is | commit | knobs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `zenav1-aom` | this repo's pure-Rust libaom port | `ea3bed3` | cq 1..63, cpu-used 0..9 |
| `svt-c` | SVT-AV1, upstream C | `dfbfe849c` (`v4.2.0-62`) | qp 0..63, preset 0..13 |
| `svt-rust` | zenav1-svt, pure-Rust SVT still-picture port | `c391e762d` | qp 0..63, preset 0..13 |
| `zenrav1e` | the rav1e fork | `4d1ed620` (`v0.1.4-109`) | quantizer 1..255, speed 0..10 |

All four are **single-threaded**, fed **byte-identical** 8-bit 4:2:0 planes, and each driver
times **only its own frame-encode call** — no process start, no file I/O, no constructor/init.
Apple M4 Pro, 12 cores, macOS; `--release`, no `-C target-cpu=native`.

Two things fall out immediately:

* **SVT-AV1 C reaches almost its whole preset ladder inside the 1 MP/s budget.** Only preset 0
  is excluded. Nothing else here is close to that.
* **SVT-AV1 presets 9-13 are aliases in all-intra.** The library prints
  `Preset M10 is mapped to M9`, and 9/10/11/12/13 are byte-identical and time-identical (230.3,
  231.5, 230.7, 230.1, 234.0 MP/s — inside the 1.6 % control band). The effective fastest
  all-intra preset is M9, for both the C encoder and the Rust port.
* `zenrav1e` speed 6 lands at **0.98 MP/s** in the 3-rep screen and **0.96 MP/s** in the 7-rep
  ladder — it misses the bar by 2-4 %, outside the 0.35 % control band for that encoder but
  close enough that a different image or size would flip it. Stated, not rounded away.

### 1b. The size axis — and why a bare "MP/s" is not a number

Four sizes per encoder per preset (64², 256², 1024², 2048² photo; 64², 256², 1024², 1856²
screen), each a Lanczos3 downscale of one source, so pixel count is the axis that moves.
Full per-cell medians in `xbench_stage1_2026-08-01.samples.tsv`; the fit and its residuals in
`xbench_stage1_fit_2026-08-01.tsv`.

At the qualifying modes, on photographic content:

| encoder | mode | 64² | 256² | 1024² | 2048² | alpha (ms) | beta (ms/MP) | max fit residual | log-log k |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| svt-c | p1 | 0.343 | 0.799 | 1.553 | 1.995 | 65.54 | 491.2 | **466 %** | 0.758 |
| svt-rust | p4 | 0.503 | 0.880 | 1.308 | 1.459 | 37.10 | 679.8 | **390 %** | 0.859 |
| zenav1-aom | cpu6 | 0.348 | 0.825 | 2.087 | 2.787 | 61.80 | 348.5 | **437 %** | 0.715 |
| zenrav1e | s7 | 0.763 | 1.146 | 1.793 | 2.218 | 43.91 | 444.8 | **752 %** | 0.846 |

(MP/s per size; alpha/beta from least squares on `total = alpha + beta * pixels`.)

**The requested alpha/beta fit is reported, and it is a bad model — that is the finding.**
The max residual column is the whole story: a straight line through the ladder over-predicts the
64² cell by 4-8x. Encode time is **sub-linear in pixel count** — the log-log exponent
`k` (time ~ pixels^k) is 0.70-0.97 everywhere, never 1.0. Per-pixel cost *falls* as the frame
grows, so:

* A single "MP/s" figure is meaningless without the size it was taken at. At the qualifying
  modes the same encoder ranges over **4-6x in MP/s** across the ladder (`zenav1-aom` cpu-used 6:
  0.348 MP/s at 64², 2.787 MP/s at 2048² — a factor of 8).
* The fitted `alpha` should NOT be read as "fixed per-call cost". It is what a straight line
  needs in order to pass near the big points, and it exceeds the entire measured 64² encode time
  in most rows. The honest statement of fixed overhead is the measured tiny cell itself:
  e.g. `svt-c` preset 13 encodes 64x64 in **0.182 ms** and 2048x2048 in **17.36 ms** — 1024x the
  pixels for only 95x the time, so the fast end is sub-linear too (k = 0.705).
* **1 MP/s is therefore a size-conditional bar.** `zenav1-aom` cpu-used 5 is 0.92 MP/s at 1 MP
  but **1.18 MP/s at 4 MP** — it fails the bar at 1 MP and passes it at 4 MP. Likewise
  `zenrav1e` speed 6: 0.96 at 1 MP, 1.15 at 4 MP. The frontier table above is pinned to the
  1 MP cell and says so.

**Is the sub-linearity an artifact of building the ladder by downscaling?** Partly — checked,
not assumed. Downscaling smooths detail, and the small cells are downscaled harder, which would
make them look *cheaper* per pixel, i.e. it works against the observed trend. A native-crop
control (same source, centre crop at native resolution, no resampling, `svt-c` preset 1,
3 timed encodes each):

| size | Lanczos3 downscale | native centre crop | native per-pixel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 256² | 84.26 ms | 123.20 ms | 1.880 µs/px |
| 1024² | 697.78 ms | 1155.91 ms | 1.102 µs/px |

Native content is 1.5-1.7x more expensive than the downscale at the same size — so the ladder
*understates* absolute cost at the large end — and the sub-linearity survives on native content
too (1.880 -> 1.102 µs/px from 256² to 1024², a 1.7x fall). The trend is real; the downscale
ladder is a lower bound on how strong it is.

### 1c. Content changes throughput more than one preset step does

Same encoders, same sizes, screen content (`gb82-sc/imac_dark`, centre-square crops) instead of
photographs, at 1 MP:

| encoder | mode | photo MP/s | screen MP/s | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| svt-c | p1 | 1.553 | 0.278 | 5.6x |
| svt-rust | p4 | 1.308 | 0.520 | 2.5x |
| zenav1-aom | cpu6 | 2.087 | 0.403 | 5.2x |
| zenrav1e | s7 | 1.793 | 0.774 | 2.3x |

**Not one of the four qualifying modes clears 1 MP/s on screen content.** For `svt-c` the photo
frontier (preset 1) runs at 0.28 MP/s on a screenshot — the screen-content frontier is
**preset 3** (1.19 MP/s; preset 2 is 0.55), two presets faster. Any ">= 1 MP/s" claim that
does not name its content class is unfalsifiable.
